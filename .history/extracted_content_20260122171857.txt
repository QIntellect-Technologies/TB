1.2. TB Transmission
1.2.1. How is TB transmied?
 Exposure to TB bacilli
TB is an airborne infecous disease and is transmied through airborne route. When a person with pulmonary or
laryngeal tuberculosis coughs, sneezes, talks, laughs or sings, the small droplets of tubercle bacilli are spread into the
air in ny droplets. Droplet nuclei can also be produced by aerosol which produces invesgaons such as sputum
inducon, bronchoscopy and through manipulaon of the lesion. Other people can breathe in these droplets and
become infected.
TB transmission mostly occurs indoors, in dark and poorly venlated spaces where the droplet nuclei stay for a
long me. Prolonged exposure to the TB bacteria is normally necessary for infecon to occur. The large pool of
paents with infecous TB leads to a high risk of exposure to the general populaon. Paents with pulmonary TB
present the main source of TB transmission. Close contact and prolonged exposure increase the risk of
transmission.
1.2.2. Risk of infecon and disease. Factors that determine the likelihood of transmission:
a) Concentraon of the bacilli
Bacilli in the index paent
Transmission of TB depends on the number of bacilli expelled into the air:
Talking (0– 210)
Coughing (3500)
Sneezing (1 000 000)
Smear posive paents are more infecous than smear negave paents, due to the high concentraon of bacilli.
Smear negave paents are expected to have reduced number of bacilli but can also transmit TB bacilli.
b) Length of exposure to TB bacilli
The length of me an exposed person breaths the contaminated air. Close contact and prolong ed exposure
increase the risk of transmission. Close contacts of paents with infecous TB including household contacts are at
a higher risk of becoming infected with TB.
c) Immune status of the exposed individual
Immuno-compromised paents are at risk of developing TB and progression from latent TB to acve TB (HIV,
immunosuppressive condions and drugs, malnutrion, alcohol). The risk of developing TB is higher in children
under the age of 5 years, elderly, miners, inmates in prisons, healthcare workers people sharing residenal
accommodaon with TB paents.
One cough can produce 3 000 droplet nuclei which can remain suspended

1.2.3. Progression of TB disease
Once infected, the progression to acve disease is dependent on the immune status of the individual. In those
with normal immunity, 90% will not progress to acve disease and only 10% will develop acve disease. People
with suppressed immunity are more likely to develop acve TB than those with normal immunity.
1.2.4. Congenital TB
Congenital TB is transmied from an infected pregnant mother through the placenta, aspiraon of infected
amnioc fluid, or ingeson of infected material during passage through the birth canal. Mortality from congenital
TB is high, esmated at 50%. Early diagnosis and commencement of treatment significantly improve survival.
1.2.5. People at risk of developing TB
Most at risk individuals include:
· Contacts
Household or close contacts are more at risk because of exposure to an index paent.
– A household contacts is someone who shared the same enclosed living space for one or more nights
or for extended periods of up to 8 hours during the day with the index paent during the 3 months
before the start of current treatment.
– A close contact is a person who is not in the household but shared an enclosed space, such as a social
gathering place, workplace or facility, for extended periods during the day with the index paent
during the 3 months before commencement of the current treatment episode
· Children < 5 years
· People living with HIV
Infecon with HIV increases the risk of progression of recent M. tuberculosis infecon and of reacvaon
of latent M. tuberculosis infecon by 5 - 15% annually. It also increases the rate of relapse and reinfecon.
· Healthcare workers, inmates in Correconal Centres, and miners
· People with condions/ on medicaons which suppress immunity such as, diabetes mellitus, silicosis,
prolonged use of corcosteroids, chemotherapy. In children, the following condions can increase the
risk of progression to TB disease, malnutrion, measles, whooping cough.
· The elderly >65 years
· Smokers
Smoking is a risk factor for TB, independent of alcohol use and other socioeconomic factors. The risk is
higher in children exposed to passive smoking. Acve smoking is associated with recurrent TB disease
and death due to TB disease. The possible biological mechanisms for the associaon between tobacco
smoke and TB are that tobacco smoke results in: (a) impaired clearance of mucosal secreons in the
tracheo-bronchial tree. This allows the M TB to reach the alveoli; (b) impaired funconing of the
pulmonary alveolar macrophages, resulng in lower levels of cytokines being secreted; (c) decreased
intracellular tumour necrosis factor- αproducon leading to impaired intracellular killing of M TB.

· Alcohol consumpon
Excessive alcohol consumpon (with or without an alcohol use disorder) is associated with three-fold risk of
developing TB. Low to medium alcohol consumpon is not associated with an increased risk of TB disease.
Alcohol use disorders are associated with clinical condions that may impair the immune system and alcohol
has a direct toxic effect on the immune system. Excessive alcohol use is also associated with poor TB
treatment adherence and several studies have found higher relapse rate among heavy drinkers and those
with alcohol use-related health disorders
1.3. Types of TB Disease
1.3.1. Pulmonary TB disease
Pulmonary TB disease involves the lung parenchyma/ssue. A paent with both a parenchymal lesion in the lungs
(PTB) and extra-pulmonary TB is classified as PTB.
1.3.2. Extra-pulmonary TB (EPTB) disease
EPTB disease involves organs other than the lungs. Intrathoracic TB such as mediasnal or hilar lymphadenopathy
or pleural effusion without a parenchymal lesion in the lungs, is classified as extra- pulmonary TB. Where several
sites are affected, the site represenng the most severe form of disease determines the case definion of extrapulmonary TB.
The organs that are commonly affected in EPTB are: pleural TB (the lining covering the lungs), TB Meningis
(central nervous system), bone and joints, lymph nodes, abdominal TB (where the liver, spleen and the intesnes
can be affected), and urogenital TB (kidney and bladder, as well as blood).
TB can be further classified as either drug suscepble TB or drug resistant TB:
Drug suscepble TB is a disease caused by MTB strain that is fully sensive to the first line TB medicines. This
means that all first line TB medicines will be effecve if they are taken properly.

Drug Resistant TB is a disease caused by MTB strain that is resistant to one or more of the TB medicines. Paents
with drug resistant TB are categorised by the resistance paern of MTB strain.

1.4. TB Infecon vs TB Disease
Infecon is the invasion of an organism's body ssues by disease-causing agents, their mulplicaon, and
reacon of host ssues to these organisms and the toxins they produce. Infecons are caused by infecous agents
including bacteria, viruses, parasites, fungi, etc.
Disease is an impairment of normal physiological funcon affecng all or part of an organism. It is a medical
condion, associated with signs and symptoms. It may be caused by factors originally from an external source,
such as infecous disease, or it may be caused by internal dysfuncons, such as autoimmune diseases.
Communicable disease refers to a disease resulng from an infecon due to pathogenic agents or toxins
generated by the infecon, following the direct or indirect transmission of the agents from the source to the host.
The pathogenic agent is also referred to as an infecous agent.

2.2. TB Risk assessment
Risk assessment should be undertaken to idenfy potenal risk areas for infecon transmission so that proper IPC
measures can be implemented. The TB risk assessment determines:
a) The risk of nosocomial transmission of TB by looking at several factors: –
b) incidence of TB in the community
c) number of paents with TB disease seen at the facility
d) melines of idenficaon, isolaon, tesng and treatment of people with TB symptoms
e) evidence of transmission at the facility
f) types of control implemented in the facility
g) The types of administrave, environmental and respiratory protecon controls that need to be
implemented in the facility.
Risk assessment also serve as a tool for ongoing evaluaon of the quality of TB infecon control measures and the
need to strengthen them. A TB risk assessment for healthcare facilies must be conducted and documented at
least annually. The TB risk assessment tool (Annexure 1) may be used as a guide for conducng a risk assessment
for a Primary Healthcare facility.
The idenfied risks from the risk assessments serve as the basis for the TB infecon prevenon and control plan.
Every facility must have a wrien TB IPC plan based on the risk assessment conducted. The plan must contain
informaon about how the facility:
a) defines employees who are at high risk of occupaonal TB exposure
b) idenfies paents with presumpve TB
c) isolates or controls exposures when a suspected or confirmed TB paent is idenfied
d) minimises the risk of exposure for employees
e) alerts the employees to hazards
f) screens employees for TB
g) uses and maintains environmental controls implemented to reduce risk of exposure
h) uses respiratory protecon
i) trains employees on TB
j) monitors the implementaon of the facility TB IPC

Definion of a TB contact
A person who has been in close contact sharing the same environment with the index paent.

Close contact: A person who is not in the household but shared an enclosed space, such as a social gathering

current treatment

2.4. TB outbreak response
TB outbreak is an observaon of more TB paents than expected in a geographic area or populaon during a
parcular me, with evidence of recent transmission of MTB among those paents. It involves mulple TB
paents with several overlapping contacts.
TB outbreak response is a process of invesgang, planning and implemenng intervenons to manage the
outbreak following suspicion of an outbreak.
TB outbreaks can be detected through laboratory data analysis and geo-mapping of confirmed TB cases, roune
TB data analysis at facility to monitor trends in TB noficaons, roune review of the contact invesgaon results,
and observaons by community members.
Confirming an outbreak involves reviewing clinical findings and laboratory results for each case, to confirm that
they had TB. Where inconsistencies exist between clinical findings for cases with laboratory results, further
informaon may be required to confirm TB disease.
Epidemiologic links among cases assist in confirming an outbreak. These are shared characteriscs between index
cases and contacts that explain where and when TB could have been transmied between them. Some contact
invesgaon findings are evidence of recent transmission, which can indicate a potenal TB outbreak.
The goal of TB outbreak response is to interrupt ongoing transmission of MTB. Contacts can be exposed to more
than one index case or can be interrelated through mulple connecons. Outbreak response requires a mul-
sectoral response including partners and stakeholders, a robust communicaon strategy, collaboraon with
community leadership and financial resources. The provincial TB Programme Manager aer consultaon with
CDC district coordinator and the district management team can declare a TB outbreak and iniate an outbreak
response.

5.9 Tuberculin Skin Test
5.9.1 Definion of Tuberculin Skin Test (TST)
The Tuberculin Skin Test (TST) is also known as the Mantoux Test. It is a skin test to determine whether a person
is infected with a bacterium, Mycobacterium and is the most reliable skin test available. The test involves
injecng tuberculin purified protein derivave (PPD) into the skin.
5.9.2 Indicaons
- To diagnose latent infecon in children and adults
- To support the diagnosis of TB in children
The test requires:
- 2 units of tuberculin purified protein derivave PPD-RT23 2TU or
- 5 units of PPD-S 5TU
5.9.3 Tuberculin Skin Test – Procedure
- A single-dose tuberculin syringe and a short 27-gauge needle with a short bevel must
be used.
- Draw up 0.1ml of PPD into the syringe.
- Clean an area of skin in the mid anterior secon of the forearm.
- The PPD is injected between layers of skin (intradermal).
- Keep the needle almost parallel to the skin, with the bevel poinng upwards during
inseron.
o It is important to ensure that the injecon goes into and not under the skin.
- A small papule should form at the injecon site; if it does not, the PPD has been
injected too deeply and the test should be repeated at a different site.
5.9.4 Reading of results
- The reacon to the test at the site of the injecon is measured 48-72 hours later by
nong the widest transverse point across the edges of the raised, thickened area.
- This area of induraon and not redness is measured.
- To help measure accurately, mark the edges of the induraon at the widest point with a
pen and measure the exact distance between the two points in millimetres.
- There are two cut off points for defining a posive tuberculin reacon: >5 mm and
>10 mm, based on the risk i.e. immune status of the paent.
- For high-risk paents such as those who are HIV posive, malnourished, with severe
illness (immunosuppressive disease or on immunosuppressive treatment), a reading ≥
5mm is considered posive.
- For all other paents, a reading of ≥ 10mm is considered posive.
